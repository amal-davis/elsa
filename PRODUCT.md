# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Everyday home cooks shopping online for traditional Indian food staples — masala and curry powders, tea powder, ready mixes, ghee, and similar pantry essentials. Many are older or less tech-savvy shoppers who want to find a familiar product, check it's the right one (weight, ingredients, freshness), and complete the purchase without friction. They're often repeat buyers restocking household basics rather than browsing for inspiration.

## Product Purpose

elsar is an e-commerce storefront for traditional food products (spice blends, curry powders, tea, ready mixes, ghee). It exists to make restocking household food staples fast, clear, and trustworthy online. Success looks like a shopper finding their product, trusting what they're buying (clear weights, ingredients, sourcing), and checking out without confusion or hesitation — the commerce mechanics (search, cart, checkout, account, order tracking) are the primary surface, not editorial storytelling.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy and efficient. Calm, clear, quietly confident — the feeling of a shop you already know will have what you need and won't waste your time. Closest to Raidco Foods' clean, credible, institutional tone: minimal visual noise, product information stated plainly, trust built through clarity rather than promotional pressure or heritage narrative. Warmth comes from precision and care in the details (accurate product info, easy reordering, legible everything), not from rustic styling or storytelling layers.

## Anti-references

- Generic templated storefronts — grid-of-identical-cards layouts that could be selling anything from electronics to clothing, with no sense of what's actually being bought
- Aggressive promotional banners, countdown timers, and discount-code popups (seen on DoubleHorse) — feels pushy and erodes the trustworthy tone
- Heavy heritage/lifestyle storytelling overlays (recipe hubs, cultural narrative sections as seen on Nirapara/DoubleHorse) competing with the core shopping task — reserve that kind of content for clearly secondary surfaces, never let it crowd the buy path

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over persuasion** — state what the product is, what it costs, and what it contains; let accuracy build trust rather than marketing pressure.
2. **Fast restocking, not discovery shopping** — optimize for the returning shopper who knows what they want: strong search, clear categories, one-tap reorder, minimal steps to checkout.
3. **Legible for every shopper** — generous touch targets, high-contrast text, simple navigation and plain-language copy that work for older and less tech-savvy users without feeling dumbed down.
4. **Specificity beats genericness** — every screen should read as "a food storefront," through product photography, units (grams/kg), ingredient and freshness details — not as an interchangeable commerce template.
5. **Calm confidence** — quiet, considered visual tone; no urgency tactics, no promotional clutter, no competing narrative layers over the shopping task.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design for older and less tech-savvy shoppers as a primary consideration: WCAG AA minimum, larger default text and touch targets, high body-text contrast (avoid light gray-on-white), simple linear flows with clear back/forward affordances, and plain-language labels and error messages over jargon or icon-only controls.
